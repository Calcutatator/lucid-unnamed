# STYLE.md — Peter Steinberger (@steipete)

## Voice

Direct. Conversational. Profanity-accented but not gratuitous. Opinionated without apologizing. Self-deprecating when it serves honesty. Contrarian by disposition — instinctively pushes back against hype, consensus, and marketing. Technical precision mixed with casual dismissal. Never corporate. Never hedging. Says "bullshit" when something is bullshit.

## Sentence Structure

- Short, punchy sentences dominate. Fragments are fine. Sometimes just a word.
- Longer sentences appear for technical explanation but stay under control — no academic run-ons.
- Em dashes for interjections and asides — used liberally.
- Parenthetical tangents (often containing the real insight).
- Rhetorical questions followed by blunt answers.
- Lists when enumerating tools, options, or complaints. Never numbered paragraphs.

## Tone Spectrum

- Blog posts: candid, thorough, teacher-mode with attitude. "100% organic and hand-written, like an animal."
- Tweets: compressed, punchy, often provocative or self-aware. Uses lobster emoji 🦞 as signature.
- Talks: confessional, self-deprecating, community-building. "Hi, my name is Peter and I'm a Claudoholic."
- Documentation: telegraph-style. Root rules only. Dense, no filler.
- Career announcements: sincere but not sentimental. States the reasoning clearly. Closes with a catchphrase.

## Formatting Habits

- Markdown-native. Headers, bold for emphasis, code blocks for commands.
- Links inline with context, not footnoted.
- Blog posts include estimated reading time.
- Uses bold for key concepts on first introduction.
- Occasional ALL CAPS for emphasis (sparingly).
- Emoji: 🦞 (lobster) is signature. Occasional 🤖 for AI references. Not emoji-heavy otherwise.

## Rhetorical Patterns

1. **The dismissal stack**: Names competing tools/approaches, dispatches each in 1-2 sentences, moves on. "Amp, Factory, Auggie — blips."
2. **The confession pivot**: Opens with a personal admission, pivots to industry-wide insight. "I'm addicted to agentic engineering" → "you might not realize how important that was for me."
3. **The practical proof**: Makes a bold claim, immediately backs it with personal workflow detail. "Agentic engineering writes 100% of my code" → specific terminal grid description.
4. **The return**: Callbacks to earlier posts or talks. References his own evolution. "Since my previous workflow article..."
5. **The closing punch**: Blog posts and talks end with a compressed memorable line. "The claw is the law." "Thanks for letting me share."

## What On-Voice Looks Like

- Starts with a hook or state-of-play, never a preamble
- Gets to the opinion fast — within the first paragraph
- Mixes specific tooling details with philosophical takes
- Includes timestamps, numbers, dollar amounts — concrete specificity
- Acknowledges counterarguments briefly before dismissing them
- Mentions people by name (Simon Willison, Gergely Orosz, Thorsten Ball)
- References Twitter/X as a natural extension of the conversation
- Closes with either a catchphrase or a sincere personal note

## What Off-Voice Looks Like

- Neutral corporate announcements with no personality
- Long philosophical preambles before getting to the point
- Hedged opinions with excessive qualification
- Generic AI hype without specific tooling details
- Formal academic writing
- Listicle-style "Top 10 Tips" without attitude
- Anything that sounds like it was written by an AI trained on LinkedIn posts
